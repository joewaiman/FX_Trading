## Per-Currency Commentary

**USD** — The clearest Strong Bull in the basket. September CPI at 3.7% YoY confirmed (BLS, Oct 14) — above the 3.6% consensus and up from 3.4% in August; core 2.6%. Energy the dominant driver (+34% YoY) as Iran conflict sustains crude prices. ISM Manufacturing hit 54.5 and Services 54.9 — marking the 9th consecutive month of services expansion. Labour market remains resilient at 4.2% unemployment. The Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00% in September; CME FedWatch prices ~42% odds of another Oct 28 hike (note: the agent found 27% for Oct 28 vs. 42% — may reflect different sources/timing; either way the bias is hawkish). Only weakness: trade deficit widening sharply (Aug: -$105.6B). Rating: 6/8 bullish.

**AUD** — The standout G10 hiker of 2026. The RBA raised to 4.60% unanimously on September 29, marking its 4th hike of the year. CPI at 4.0% remains well above the 2–3% target; trimmed mean 3.6% (also above). Manufacturing PMI 52.0, Services 53.2 — both in expansion. The main risk is unemployment, which has been edging higher all year to 4.7% in September (above the 4.6% forecast). GDP growth of +2.1% YoY is modest but positive. Q3 CPI due October 28 is the next major catalyst. AUD outperformed vs USD in W42 (-0.32% on the week, underperforming relative to its rate advantage — catch-up potential).

**EUR** — Significant upgrade from W22's Mild Bearish. S&P Global composite PMI hit 53.1 in September — a 41-month high, signalling genuine economic momentum. Both manufacturing output (53.4) and services (53.0) are in expansion. CPI re-accelerated to 3.8% YoY in September, forcing ECB to maintain hawkish signals (held at 2.50%; Oct 29 decision is live). The caution flags: trade balance is a -€16.1B deficit (widening), and rate at 2.50% is still 125–150bp below the Fed. The ECB hiking more aggressively would be bullish for EUR; a hold with dovish guidance would weaken. For now: Mild Bullish on PMI momentum, not a conviction long.

**NOK** — Mild Bullish, oil-amplified. Norges Bank raised to 4.50% on September 24 — among the highest policy rates in G10. Brent at ~$105/barrel directly translates into Norway's enormous petroleum revenues; the YTD trade surplus stands at NOK 502 billion. CPI at 3.4% above the 2% target with core at 3.0% maintains the hawkish case. The weakness: mainland (non-oil) GDP growth is modest at ~+1%, and the PMI series was discontinued. NOK/SEK is the highest-conviction pair in the basket given the 275bp rate differential and oil tailwind.

**JPY** — Downgraded from W22's Mild Bullish to Neutral. The BoJ did hike to 1.25% on September 18 — a notable milestone — and Governor Ueda reiterated hawkish intent in W42. However, core CPI (ex-fresh food) is only 1.7% YoY — still below the 2% target, which limits urgency for further hikes. Manufacturing PMI remains strong at 54.1 and retail sales have been positive (+1.3% last reported). But USD/JPY at 158 signals the market is unconvinced: the 275bp US-Japan rate differential continues to drive carry flows. JPY will only re-rate when markets simultaneously price Fed cuts + BoJ hikes. Not there yet. Sep CPI (Oct 22) and BoJ Oct 28–29 meeting are catalysts.

**CHF** — Downgraded from W22's Mild Bullish to Mild Bearish. The SNB held at 0% on September 24, leaving Switzerland with the lowest policy rate in this universe. Inflation at 0.8% is within the 0–2% target with no urgency to hike; markets price ~50% odds of a hike only by December 2026 / early 2027. The rate differential is deeply negative vs every peer: -375–400bp vs USD, -460bp vs AUD, -450bp vs Norges Bank. Safe-haven demand (Iran tensions) provides a floor, partially offsetting the carry disadvantage. USD/CHF is the highest-conviction long in the basket on rate differential alone.

**GBP** — Neutral with imminent upgrade potential. The BoE held at 3.75% on September 17 with a 6-3 vote — three members voted for an immediate hike to 4.00%. Reuters surveys show 54/63 economists expect a November 5 hike. UK GDP Q2 +0.5% QoQ is positive but modest. Unemployment at 4.9% (May–Jul) is within the neutral band. PMIs are in low expansion: Mfg 51.9, Services 52.1. The key catalyst is UK September CPI due **October 22** — if it comes in above the 3.1% August print, the hawk case builds rapidly. Run GBP/USD and EUR/GBP technical analysis after the print.

**NZD** — Neutral, watch October 21. A critical data correction from W26 context: the RBNZ is NOT in a cutting cycle — it raised to 2.75% on September 2. The October 28 meeting is described as "completely open" with an upside lean. Q3 CPI (due **October 21**) is the decisive near-term catalyst: if above the 1–3% target, a November hike becomes likely → NZD upgrade to Mild Bullish. Unemployment at 5.6% Q2 (10-year high) is the bearish offset. GDP +2.6% YoY is resilient. Wait for the CPI print.

**CAD** — Neutral, leaning negative near-term. September jobs were -68,000 — a major miss, the largest decline in years — which has shifted BoC expectations toward a cut at the October 28 meeting. However, the Ivey PMI surged to 58.2 in September (strong expansion) and the August trade surplus was C$4.2B (4-year high, driven by energy exports ahead of potential US tariffs). BoC is at 2.25%. **Sep CPI on October 19** is the make-or-break: a hot print could prevent a cut and shift CAD to Mild Bullish; an in-line print likely seals an October cut.

**SEK** — Mild Bearish, the structural underperformer. Riksbank at 1.75% is the second-lowest rate in G10 (above only SNB at 0%). CPIF at 1.5% in September is below the 2% target — the opposite problem from most peers. Unemployment at 8.7% is the highest in this universe. Q2 GDP at +1.6% QoQ is a bright spot, as is retail growth, but these are insufficient to offset the rate/inflation negatives. SEK's W42 outperformance (+0.86% vs USD — the week's best) was driven by EUR/USD strength and global risk-on, not independent SEK fundamentals. USD/SEK is a high-conviction long above current levels.
